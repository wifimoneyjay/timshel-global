## Retro-commissioning: findings and proposed service language

The U.S. Department of Energy distinguishes retro-commissioning from recommissioning: retro-commissioning applies commissioning to existing buildings that did not receive it originally; recommissioning repeats the process in buildings previously commissioned. Both address how systems operate together and how well they meet present needs. This supports replacing the old branded service with a clearly described existing-building service, without transferring any licensing claims. [DOE: Commissioning Federal Buildings](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/commissioning-federal-buildings)

The DOE/SEE Action guide describes four stages: planning, investigation, implementation, and handoff. The investigation can examine schedules, setpoints, controls sequences, sensor operation, and system performance. Corrections should be prioritized, implemented, and verified; operating documentation and staff training help preserve results. This provides the basis for the service deliverables below. Outcomes depend on the building and measures implemented, so the previous percentage-savings claim is removed. [DOE/SEE Action: Energy Audits and Retro-Commissioning Policy Guide](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-07/commercialbuildings_audits_rcx_policy_guide.pdf)

The Building Commissioning Association also emphasizes documenting current facility requirements and using those requirements to evaluate existing buildings. This reinforces a process based on an owner's current needs rather than simply assuming the original design remains appropriate. [BCxA: Getting the Most Out of Your Toolbox](https://www.bcxa.org/blog/getting-the-most-out-of-your-toolbox.html)

### Homepage copy

**Retro-Commissioning**

Improve existing building performance through system testing, controls adjustments, and verification of corrective measures.

### Services page copy

Retro-commissioning evaluates existing buildings that were not commissioned during construction. It identifies how equipment and controls perform together under today's operating needs. We investigate comfort complaints, scheduling problems, and energy waste through document review, trend analysis, and functional testing.

Our process establishes current facility requirements, prioritizes practical corrections, and verifies the results. Findings, updated operating sequences, and staff guidance give owners a clear plan for maintaining building performance.

Deliverables:

- Current Facility Requirements
- Retro-Commissioning Plan
- Controls Trend Analysis & Functional Testing
- Prioritized Findings & Corrective Measures
- Implementation Verification Report
- Operating Documentation & Staff Training

The copy does not claim a Texas A&M license, affiliation, proprietary methodology, or guaranteed savings. Related licensing badges, the About-page partnership section, and the old metadata service name are removed for consistency. Existing unrelated qualifications remain pending the owner's updated credentials.
